        Part 1: Where Do I Start?

        I am putting together a step-by-step guide for international medical graduates who want to practice medicine in the United States.

        Save these posts. I will take you from your foreign medical school all the way to practicing medicine in America.

        First, understand something important:

        There is no longer only one route.

        The traditional pathway is:

        Foreign medical school → USMLE → ECFMG Certification → U.S. clinical experience → ERAS → Match → U.S. residency → state license → practice

        But experienced physicians should also know that a growing number of U.S. states have created additional pathways for internationally trained doctors. Some qualifying physicians may eventually obtain a license without repeating an entire U.S. residency.

        STEP 1: CHECK YOUR MEDICAL SCHOOL

        Before paying for USMLE courses, rotations or applications, make sure your medical school qualifies.

        Search the official World Directory of Medical Schools:

        https://search.wdoms.org/

        Find your school.

        Then look at the Sponsor Notes section.

        You need the appropriate ECFMG Sponsor Note covering your graduation year.

        Simply finding your medical school in the World Directory does not automatically mean you are eligible for ECFMG Certification.

        STEP 2: START WITH ECFMG / INTEALTH

        Official ECFMG information:

        https://www.ecfmg.org/certification/

        MyIntealth:

        https://www.myintealth.org/

        You will establish your identity and begin the ECFMG certification process.

        Your medical education credentials ultimately need to be verified.

        Do this early. Credential verification can take time.

        International medical students who want U.S. residency

        Older graduates

        Practicing physicians outside the United States

        Foreign-trained specialists

        Doctors who need a U.S. visa

        Part 2: ECFMG Certification

        ECFMG Certification is one of the main gateways for international medical graduates entering U.S. residency. Here it is in plain English.

        1. CONFIRM YOUR MEDICAL SCHOOL

        World Directory: https://search.wdoms.org/

        Check the Sponsor Notes for your graduation year.

        2. OPEN YOUR ECFMG / INTEALTH ACCOUNT AND APPLY

        https://www.ecfmg.org/

        https://www.myintealth.org/

        Your medical credentials get verified. Start early, it takes time.

        3. PASS USMLE STEP 1

        Step 1 is Pass/Fail.

        https://www.usmle.org/

        4. PASS USMLE STEP 2 CK

        Step 2 CK gives a numerical score and matters a lot in residency selection.

        5. COMPLETE A PATHWAY (this replaced Step 2 CS)

        Step 2 CS no longer exists. For 2027 there are six Pathways. They cover your clinical skills and communication skills.

        Pathway 1: you hold, or recently held, a license to practice medicine without supervision in another country. If you qualify for Pathway 1, you must use it.

        Pathway 2: your medical school gives an OSCE that is required for licensure in your country.

        Pathways 3, 4, 5: your school is accredited by a WFME-recognized agency, an NCFMEA-comparable agency, or issues a joint degree with a U.S. LCME school.

        Pathway 6: for everyone who does not qualify for 1 to 5. Licensed physicians evaluate your clinical skills with the Mini-CEX.

        If you failed Step 2 CS in the past, you must use Pathway 6.

        Details: https://www.ecfmg.org/certification-pathways/

        6. PASS OET MEDICINE

        Every Pathways applicant must get a satisfactory OET Medicine score. No exceptions, regardless of native language or where you studied.

        https://oet.com/

        7. SUBMIT YOUR PATHWAY APPLICATION

        A good OET score alone is not enough. ECFMG must review and approve your Pathway application.

        THINGS PEOPLE MISS

        • Seven-year rule: you must complete all exam requirements within seven years of your first passed exam.
        • Pathways can expire. A Pathway-based ECFMG Certificate expires when the Pathway expires, unless you revalidate it or qualify for an indefinitely valid certificate.
        • For the Match, all exam requirements must be done by the NRMP Rank Order List certification deadline.
        • ECFMG Certification is NOT a medical license.

        Part 3: U.S. Clinical Experience

        One of the most confusing subjects for IMGs is "USCE."

        Not all U.S. clinical experiences are equal.

        ELECTIVE

        Usually intended for someone who is still a medical student.

        Depending on the institution, you may participate in supervised patient care.

        If you are still in medical school, investigate electives before graduating.

        This can be one of your best opportunities to obtain meaningful U.S. clinical experience and U.S. Letters of Recommendation.

        Start with AAMC's Visiting Student Learning Opportunities:

        https://students-residents.aamc.org/visiting-student-learning-opportunities/visiting-student-learning-opportunities-vslo

        OBSERVERSHIP

        Generally means observing/shadowing physicians.

        You are not independently treating patients.

        Observerships can still help you:

        understand the U.S. medical system

        meet physicians

        understand documentation and workflow

        demonstrate professionalism

        potentially develop relationships for LORs

        AMA has an excellent IMG observership guide:

        https://www.ama-assn.org/member-groups-sections/international-medical-graduates-section-imgs/finding-observership-faqs

        EXTERNSHIP

        Be careful with this term.

        Different organizations use "externship" differently.

        Before paying, ask exactly what you will be permitted to do.

        BEFORE PAYING ANY USCE COMPANY, ASK:

        1. What hospital or clinic will I actually attend?

        2. Who is my supervising physician?

        3. Is there direct patient interaction?

        4. Is the physician affiliated with a residency program?

        5. How many students rotate simultaneously?

        6. Will I receive an evaluation?

        7. Can I receive an individualized LOR?

        8. Will the letter identify where I rotated?

        9. Does the institution itself know I am coming?

        10. Can I independently verify everything before paying?

        Never pay thousands of dollars just because an advertisement says:

        "Guaranteed USCE + guaranteed LOR."

        Verify it.

        Part 4: Finding Residency

        Do not blindly apply to 200 residency programs.

        Research them.

        Three websites should be in every IMG's bookmarks:

        FREIDA

        AMA residency/fellowship database:

        https://freida.ama-assn.org/

        RESIDENCY EXPLORER

        Compare residency programs and applicant characteristics:

        https://www.residencyexplorer.org/

        ERAS

        This is the AAMC application system:

        https://students-residents.aamc.org/applying-residencies-eras/applying-residencies-eras

        Look at:

        Does the program accept IMGs?

        Does it have current IMG residents?

        Graduation-year restrictions?

        USMLE attempt restrictions?

        Step 2 expectations?

        USCE requirements?

        J-1 sponsorship?

        H-1B sponsorship?

        Step 3 requirement for H-1B?

        Specialty-specific requirements?

        BUILD YOUR APPLICATION

        A competitive IMG application may include:

        ✓ Step 1 pass

        ✓ Strong Step 2 CK performance

        ✓ ECFMG requirements completed on time

        ✓ Meaningful USCE

        ✓ Strong U.S. LORs

        ✓ Personal Statement

        ✓ MSPE

        ✓ Medical school transcript

        ✓ Research/publications where relevant

        ✓ Clear explanation for significant gaps

        ✓ Strategic program selection

        ERAS IS NOT THE MATCH

        This confuses many first-time applicants.

        ERAS = sends your residency applications.

        NRMP = runs the Match.

        You need to register separately with NRMP:

        https://www.nrmp.org/

        For the current 2027 Match cycle, the application season is already underway.

        Do not wait until the last minute to understand the process.

        Ask:

        Was my Step 2 CK competitive for the programs I selected?

        Did I apply too narrowly?

        Was my year of graduation a problem?

        Were there examination attempts?

        Did I have enough meaningful USCE?

        Were my LORs actually strong?

        Did I apply to programs that routinely interview IMGs?

        Did I need visa sponsorship?

        Did I apply late?

        Was my Personal Statement generic?

        Did I have unexplained clinical gaps?

        Did I receive interviews but rank poorly?

        Those represent different problems and require different solutions.

        Part 6: Practicing Without Repeating Residency

        This answer is changing.

        For decades, an experienced foreign physician could have completed residency, specialty training and years of independent practice overseas and still face repeating U.S. postgraduate training.

        Several states have now created additional pathways for internationally trained physicians.

        As of the 2026 FSMB analysis, 18 states had enacted additional IMG/internationally trained physician pathways:

        Arkansas

        Florida

        Idaho

        Illinois

        Indiana

        Iowa

        Louisiana

        Massachusetts

        Minnesota

        Nevada

        North Carolina

        Oklahoma

        Oregon

        Rhode Island

        Tennessee

        Texas

        Virginia

        Wisconsin

        This does NOT mean you can simply move to one of these states and start practicing.

        Every state has different requirements.

        Depending upon the state, requirements can include:

        ECFMG Certification

        USMLE examinations

        Completed foreign residency

        Several years of practice abroad

        Foreign board certification

        Unrestricted foreign license

        Recent clinical practice

        Credential verification

        U.S. employer/hospital sponsorship

        Provisional medical license

        Supervised U.S. practice

        Service in designated locations

        Malpractice insurance

        Background checks

        Transition to full licensure after successful supervised practice

        Some pathways are much more restrictive than others.

        And implementation can lag behind passage of the law.

        IF YOU ARE AN EXPERIENCED FOREIGN PHYSICIAN:

        Do this BEFORE deciding to repeat residency:

        1. Collect proof of your foreign residency.

        2. Collect your specialty/board credentials.

        3. Document your medical licenses.

        4. Document your years of independent practice.

        5. Document recent clinical activity.

        6. Check ECFMG requirements.

        7. Review the state's alternative IMG law.

        8. Contact the state medical board directly.

        9. Determine whether an employer must sponsor you.

        10. Separately determine your immigration/work-authorization route.

        FSMB:

        https://www.fsmb.org/

        Find individual state medical boards:

        https://www.fsmb.org/contact-a-state-medical-board/

        Licensing and immigration are separate problems.

        Being eligible for a state medical license does not automatically give you permission to work in America.

        Next:

        State laws are changing rapidly. Verify everything with the individual state medical board before making financial or career decisions.

        Part 7: Visas for Doctors

        USMLE and immigration are two completely different systems.

        Passing USMLE does not give you a visa.

        ECFMG Certification does not give you permission to work.

        Even medical-license eligibility does not automatically provide work authorization.

        Here are the major routes you should understand.

        B-1/B-2

        May be appropriate for certain short observational activities, depending upon exactly what you will be doing.

        Do NOT assume visitor status permits clinical employment or hands-on medical practice.

        J-1 PHYSICIAN

        Extremely common for IMG residency/fellowship.

        ECFMG/Intealth sponsors J-1 physicians participating in clinical graduate medical education.

        Official information:

        https://www.ecfmg.org/evsp/

        J-1 physicians are generally subject to the two-year home-country physical-presence requirement.

        That is why you should understand J-1 waivers before finishing training.

        CONRAD 30

        One important J-1 waiver pathway.

        States can recommend qualifying physicians for waivers, generally involving service to underserved populations.

        A qualifying position generally involves full-time service for at least three years.

        Official waiver information:

        https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/study/exchange/waiver-of-the-exchange-visitor.html

        H-1B

        Some residency programs and physician employers sponsor H-1B status.

        Not all do.

        For physicians, USMLE Step 3 can become particularly important for H-1B eligibility.

        If you require H-1B sponsorship, investigate this BEFORE applying/ranking programs.

        O-1A

        Potential route for physicians meeting the legal standard for extraordinary ability.

        Particularly relevant to some highly accomplished clinicians, researchers and academics.

        PERMANENT RESIDENCE / GREEN CARD

        Potential pathways can include:

        EB-1A extraordinary ability

        EB-2 National Interest Waiver

        Physician National Interest Waiver

        PERM/employer-sponsored immigration

        family-based immigration where independently eligible

        A physician NIW has special rules involving qualifying medical service.

        USCIS:

        https://www.uscis.gov/

        Department of State visas:

        https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas.html

        IMPORTANT

        Your best medical pathway and your best immigration pathway need to be designed together.

        Don't wait until the end of residency to discover that an immigration decision you made years earlier affects what you can do next.
